How the two-week notice date is calculated

        
The default method adds 14 calendar days to the date you give notice. For example, if you give notice on a Monday, the estimated final working day is two Mondays later.

        Read the full resignation guides
        
Some teams count the last day differently, especially when holidays, shift work, PTO, contracts, or company policy are involved. Use this as a planning estimate and confirm the date in writing.

      

      

        
Should weekends count?

        
In everyday use, two weeks notice usually means two calendar weeks, so weekends are included. If your workplace schedules around business days, use the business-day option to compare a weekday-only count such as 10 business days for two weeks.

        
The important thing is clarity: your notice should state the exact final working day instead of relying only on the phrase "two weeks notice."

      

    


    

      
2 week notice calculator FAQ

      

        Is two weeks notice legally required?
        
It depends on your job, contract, location, and employer policy. In many situations it is a professional courtesy rather than a universal legal rule. Check your agreement or ask HR if the stakes are high.

      

      

        What if my last day falls on a weekend?
        
If you normally work Monday-Friday, you may choose the closest regular workday and confirm it with your manager. The calculator shows both the date and the following day so you can discuss the transition clearly.

      

      

        Can I give more than two weeks notice?
        
Yes. Many people give more notice for senior roles, specialized work, or long handoffs. Use the notice length dropdown to compare one, two, three, and four-week periods.

      

      

        Should I put my last working day in the resignation letter?
        
Yes. A specific final date prevents confusion and gives your employer a clear transition timeline.
